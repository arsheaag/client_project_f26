During Your Engaged Learning Experience

Navigating Your Active Project

Once your project is underway, your team will need to balance project goals with new information, feedback, and changing circumstances. Regular communication, thoughtful research, and flexible project management can help your team respond effectively while maintaining a strong relationship with your project partner.

Stay on Track

Engaged learning projects do not always follow the original plan. During the experience, your team should continue learning from stakeholders, communicating with your partner, tracking progress, and adjusting the work when new needs or challenges emerge.

Manage the Project Adaptively

  • Track progress: Regularly review project goals, responsibilities, milestones, and deadlines.
  • Communicate regularly: Keep your team and project partner informed about progress, questions, decisions, and challenges.
  • Respond to change: Revisit the project plan when new information, constraints, or stakeholder needs emerge.
  • Document decisions: Keep track of important changes and the reasoning behind project decisions.

Conduct Human-Centered Fieldwork

Research and fieldwork can help your team understand the people, activities, environments, interactions, and needs connected to the project. Approach this work with curiosity and avoid assuming that your team's initial understanding of the problem is complete.

  • Observe the context in which people interact with the problem.
  • Ask questions and listen carefully to stakeholder experiences.
  • Record observations and emerging insights.
  • Look for patterns across multiple sources of information.
  • Use what you learn to reconsider assumptions and project decisions.

Keep Your Partner Updated

Regular sponsor or client updates help maintain alignment throughout the project. Do not wait until the final deliverable to show your progress or raise concerns.

  • Share progress toward important milestones.
  • Identify questions or decisions that require partner input.
  • Communicate challenges before they become larger problems.
  • Confirm whether project priorities or expectations have changed.
  • End meetings with clear responsibilities and next steps.

Use Feedback as Part of the Process

Feedback should happen throughout the project rather than only at the end. Sharing work in progress gives your team an opportunity to test assumptions, identify problems, and make changes while there is still time to improve the outcome.

  1. Share work or ideas early enough for feedback to influence them.
  2. Ask specific questions about areas where your team needs input.
  3. Listen carefully and document the feedback you receive.
  4. Discuss what the feedback means for the project.
  5. Decide what changes should be made and communicate those decisions.

Questions to Consider During the Project

  • Are we still addressing the partner's most important needs?
  • Are our project goals and timeline still realistic?
  • What have we learned that challenges our original assumptions?
  • Are we communicating often enough with our project partner?
  • Whose perspectives are represented in our research and decisions?
  • What feedback have we received, and how are we responding to it?
  • What challenges need to be discussed before we continue?

Featured During-the-Experience Resources

Use these resources to support research, communication, feedback, and project management throughout your engaged learning experience.

  • HFLI AEIOU Worksheet: A framework for organizing observations around activities, environments, interactions, objects, and users.
  • User-Needs-Insights Worksheet: A resource for translating research observations into user needs and project insights.
  • Feedback Worksheet: A tool for collecting and organizing feedback on work in progress.
  • Ginsberg Community Engagement Cases: Cases that support reflection and decision-making in community-engaged work.
  • Sponsor Dashboard Template: A resource for communicating project progress and important updates with a sponsor or client.
  • Student Project Communications Plan: A resource for planning how the team will communicate with project stakeholders.
  • Project Management Worksheet: A resource for tracking project scope, objectives, team roles, timelines, and progress.
