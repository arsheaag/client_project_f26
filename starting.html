Starting Your Engaged Learning Experience

Build a Strong Foundation

Starting an engaged learning experience means turning preparation into action. Early communication, clear expectations, and thoughtful relationship-building can help your team and project partner begin the experience with a shared understanding of the work ahead.

Starting the Partnership

The beginning of a project is an important time for establishing professional communication and learning how your team and partner will work together. Initial meetings should focus on listening, asking questions, clarifying expectations, and building a shared understanding of the project.

Starting Goals

  • Establish professional communication: Decide how and when your team will communicate with the project partner.
  • Conduct effective first meetings: Prepare questions and an agenda so your team can learn about the partner, project context, needs, and expectations.
  • Clarify roles and responsibilities: Make sure team members understand what they are responsible for and how work will be coordinated.
  • Confirm the project direction: Discuss the project's goals, scope, timeline, constraints, and expected outcomes with your partner.
  • Formalize agreements: Document expectations and commitments so that the team and partner have a shared understanding of the relationship.

Preparing for Your First Partner Meeting

Your first meetings with a project partner can shape the relationship for the rest of the experience. Arrive prepared to listen and learn rather than immediately proposing solutions.

  1. Review what your team already knows about the organization and project.
  2. Create an agenda and identify the most important questions to discuss.
  3. Introduce team members and clarify everyone's role.
  4. Ask the partner about their priorities, needs, constraints, and desired outcomes.
  5. Discuss communication preferences and how often the team should provide updates.
  6. Record decisions, unanswered questions, and next steps after the meeting.

Questions to Discuss with Your Partner

  • What does success look like for this project?
  • Who are the primary stakeholders or users affected by the work?
  • What constraints should our team understand?
  • What deliverables are expected from the project?
  • How would you prefer our team to communicate progress and questions?
  • Who should make decisions when the project direction needs to change?
  • Are there important deadlines or milestones we should plan around?

Before Moving Forward

Before beginning major project work, make sure your team and partner share an understanding of the project's scope, responsibilities, communication practices, and next steps. Documenting these decisions early can make it easier to respond when the project changes later.
